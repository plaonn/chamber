# Chamber trademark and brand policy

Chamber is open-source software, but the software license and the project's brand identity are separate.

The repository is licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0 unless otherwise noted. Section 6 of Apache-2.0 does not grant permission to use a licensor's trade names, trademarks, service marks, or product names except for reasonable and customary descriptive use.

## Project identifiers

The `Chamber` name and the official Chamber symbol, logo, wordmark, and other source-identifying brand assets are project identifiers for purposes of this policy.

This policy does **not** state or imply that any Chamber mark is registered in any jurisdiction. It is a lightweight project policy intended to keep open-source code permissions separate from branding and endorsement.

The canonical symbol and its reproducible source are documented under `brand/`.

## Uses that normally do not need separate permission

You may use the Chamber name or marks in a truthful, non-confusing way to:

- refer to the Chamber project or link to this repository;
- describe compatibility or integration, such as `works with Chamber` or `for Chamber`;
- include screenshots or excerpts in documentation, reviews, tutorials, comparisons, or commentary;
- describe a modified distribution as a fork of Chamber, provided the fork is clearly identified as unofficial and uses a distinct product identity.

These uses must not imply sponsorship, endorsement, certification, or official status that does not exist.

## Uses that require separate permission

Please obtain permission before using the Chamber name or official marks in a way that could identify your own product, service, organization, or distribution as the official Chamber project, including:

- using `Chamber` as the primary name of a modified or independently distributed product in a confusing way;
- using the official Chamber symbol or logo as your own product or organization identity;
- presenting a modified mark as an official Chamber mark;
- using a package, domain, account, organization, or service name that is likely to make users believe it is an official Chamber distribution or service;
- claiming sponsorship, endorsement, certification, or partnership without approval.

A fork can always describe its origin factually, for example `a fork of Chamber`, while using a distinct name and branding for the fork itself.

## Canonical and modified marks

Canonical Chamber brand assets are defined by `brand/README.md`, `brand/brand-spec.json`, the generator, and the generated canonical exports. Modifying those files does not automatically make the resulting mark an official Chamber mark.

Brand changes merged into the canonical repository become official only through normal maintainer review of the corresponding brand specification and exports.

## Administration

For now, brand permissions are administered by the maintainer/repository owner through the `plaonn/chamber` project. This is intentionally lightweight: there is no trademark-registration, CLA, or corporate-ownership requirement attached to ordinary use or contribution at this stage.

If Chamber later becomes a commercial product, develops a larger contributor ecosystem, or encounters confusing third-party branding, this policy and the need for formal trademark registration can be revisited.
